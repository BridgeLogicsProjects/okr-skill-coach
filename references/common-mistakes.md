# 10 Common OKR Mistakes and How to Fix Them

## 1. Objectives that describe tasks, not direction
**Mistake:** "Complete the product redesign"
**Fix:** Ask — what does the redesign make possible? Write that instead.
"Create an experience so intuitive that users recommend us without being asked"

## 2. Key Results that measure output, not outcome
**Mistake:** "Launch 3 new features"
**Fix:** Ask — what changes for the user because of those features?
"Increase feature engagement from 20% to 45% of active users"

## 3. Too many OKRs
**Mistake:** 6 objectives, 18 key results
**Fix:** Maximum 3 objectives per cycle. If everything is a priority, nothing is.

## 4. OKRs written without the team
**Mistake:** Leadership writes OKRs and hands them down
**Fix:** Co-create with the people doing the work. Ownership drives execution.

## 5. No baseline
**Mistake:** "Increase retention" with no starting number
**Fix:** Find the baseline first. No baseline means no way to know if you moved.

## 6. Confidence too high
**Mistake:** "We are 95% confident we will hit this"
**Fix:** If confidence is above 80%, the target is not ambitious enough. Aim for 60 to 70%.

## 7. No check-in rhythm
**Mistake:** OKRs set in January, reviewed in December
**Fix:** Every two weeks, update confidence level in an existing team meeting. OKRs without rhythm are wishes.

## 8. Mixing strategy and execution in the same OKR
**Mistake:** Objective is "Build the AI roadmap and ship 4 features and hire 2 engineers"
**Fix:** One objective, one direction. Separate OKRs for separate problems.

## 9. Key Results that cannot be measured
**Mistake:** "Improve team morale"
**Fix:** Find a proxy. "Increase team satisfaction score from 6.2 to 8.0 in quarterly pulse survey"

## 10. Forgetting the why
**Mistake:** OKRs exist but nobody knows why they matter this cycle
**Fix:** Always connect OKRs to the strategy. If you cannot explain why this objective matters to the company's direction, rewrite it or remove it.
